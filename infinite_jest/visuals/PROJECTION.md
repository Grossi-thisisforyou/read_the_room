# INFINITE JEST: Projection Strategy

One projector, one image, one visitor at a time. This page covers where the image goes, how big, how bright, and what to check before Nov 23. Numbers marked **(est.)** are estimates; measure them in the room.

---

## 1. The surface

The image we want is Bacon's: one figure, alone, on a flat saturated field, with thin cage lines around it. The visitor should meet their distorted double at roughly their own size.

**Size rule:** the Pi outputs 16:9. To show a standing figure about 1.8 m tall, the image needs to be at least about 1.7 m high, which means **about 3.0 m wide**. At 3.5 m wide the image is 1.97 m high and the figure can be fully life-size with headroom above.

| Option | Good for this piece | Problems |
|---|---|---|
| Bare wall | Free, big, already flat | Taupe absorbs light (dull, muddy reds); fire strobe and pull station limit where it can go |
| Freestanding or hung flat (white or light grey) | Clean edges like a painting; you choose where it stands, away from windows and fire gear | Needs a frame or stand; costs money |
| Rear projection on scrim / tulle | Figure "floats"; no projector visible; visitor can walk up close without shadows | Tulle lets most light pass through, so the image is faint; needs real darkness and 1.5 to 3.5 m of space behind it |
| Figure-height panel (body-sized, about 0.8 x 2 m) | Very intimate, like a person standing there | Loses Bacon's big flat field; hard to aim; tiny target |

**Primary: front projection onto a flat, matte, white or light grey surface about 3 m wide**, bottom edge 20 to 30 cm above the floor so the figure stands on the "same floor" as the visitor. A painted plywood flat or a matte screen works. Position it on a wall that does not face the windows and is well clear of the fire strobe, pull station and stair exit.

**Fallback: a 2.5 m wide image directly on the wall**, accepting a figure about 1.4 m tall (slightly smaller than life, still strong). If the only usable wall is the taupe one, keep the image at least 1 m from the fire equipment and do not cover the exit path.

Rear projection is the dream version but only if you get full blackout and a proper rear-projection screen material (not tulle).

## 2. Throw math and projector position

**Throw distance = throw ratio x image width.** The throw ratio is printed in the projector's spec sheet (often a range if it has a zoom lens).

| Image width (height) | Ratio 0.5 (short throw) | Ratio 1.2 | Ratio 1.5 | Ratio 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 m (1.13 m) | 1.0 m | 2.4 m | 3.0 m | 4.0 m |
| 2.5 m (1.41 m) | 1.25 m | 3.0 m | 3.75 m | 5.0 m |
| 3.0 m (1.69 m) | 1.5 m | 3.6 m | 4.5 m | 6.0 m |
| 3.5 m (1.97 m) | 1.75 m | 4.2 m | 5.25 m | 7.0 m (whole room, not workable) |

The room is about 7 m long, so ratios of 1.2 to 1.5 at 3 m wide fit comfortably. A 2.0 ratio projector will struggle.

**Where it sits:**

- **Short throw (0.5):** on the floor or a low plinth 1.5 m in front of the image, pointing up. The visitor almost never blocks it. Clean, but you lose the shadow option.
- **Standard throw (1.2 to 1.5):** high up behind the visitor (a tall stand or shelf at 2.2 m or more), 3.6 to 4.5 m from the image. A visitor standing 1.5 to 2.5 m from the image will cast a shadow on its lower half.

**The shadow as a design choice.** The visitor's own shadow inside the image is a strong intimacy move: their dark body sits in the same field as their painted double, like the black pools Bacon puts under his figures. If you want it, mount the projector high and slightly off-centre so the shadow falls beside the figure rather than erasing it. Mark a spot on the floor (tape, a small light) to guide where people stand. If you do not want it, use short throw. Either way, place the projector so no one has to look into the lens.

## 3. Daylight and brightness

**The key fact:** a projector cannot make black. Projected "black" is just the wall, lit by whatever light is in the room. Bacon's dark grounds and every shadow in the image are the first things to disappear in a bright room.

**Rough math (est.):**

- Wall brightness from room light (nits) ≈ lux on wall x 0.8 / 3.14 for a white wall.
- Image brightness (nits) ≈ projector lumens x screen gain / (3.14 x image area in m²).
- Aim for image white at least **10 times** the wall's own brightness for dark imagery, and never below **5 times** if text must read.

| Room condition | Light on wall (est.) | Wall "black" (est.) | Image white needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daylight through windows, pendants on | 300 to 1000 lux | 75 to 250 nits | 400+ nits (impractical) |
| Dusk or blinds, a few lamps | about 50 lux | about 13 nits | about 150 nits |
| After sunset, pendants off, windows covered | 5 to 10 lux | 1 to 3 nits | about 50 nits |

**Rated ANSI lumens to buy or borrow** (white screen, gain 1.0, includes a 1.4x margin for eco mode and lamp age, est.):

| Image width | Dark room (50 nits) | Dim room (150 nits) | Daylight (300 nits) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 m | 500 | 1,500 | 3,000 |
| 2.5 m | 800 | 2,300 | 4,600 |
| 3.0 m | 1,100 | 3,300 | 6,700 |
| 3.5 m | 1,500 | 4,500 | 9,100 |

**Recommendation:** for a 3 m image, **at least 3,000 to 4,000 ANSI lumens** if any room light remains, or **about 1,500** with real blackout after dark. Projecting into full daylight is not worth fighting.

**Ways to win the darkness:**

- **Schedule after sunset.** Sunset is about 4:32 pm on Nov 23, 4:28 pm on Dec 9 and 4:29 pm on Dec 13. Real darkness arrives about 30 minutes later, so **start showings at 5:00 pm or later.**
- **Pendants:** switch them off, or wrap one in a deep red or amber gel so it becomes part of the piece and stays off the image.
- **Windows:** black molton or duvetyne curtains (heavy, fire-rated fabric; ask the building about fire certification), or temporary blackout window film. Even covering only the windows facing the image helps a lot.
- **Screen:** a grey ambient-light-rejecting (ALR) screen rejects side light and makes blacks deeper; good if blackout is partial. A matte light-grey painted flat is the cheap version.
- **Avoid the taupe wall** for the image if possible: it eats about a third of the light (est.) and shifts reds toward brown.

## 4. Making one image hold the room

**Composition rules:**

- Keep the figure **centred and at eye height** (head at about 1.6 m from the floor).
- **Flat saturated grounds** (Bacon orange, acid green, deep pink) survive room light far better than dark gradients. Use darkness as accent, not as ground, unless the room is truly black.
- **Text:** white on black or black on a flat colour, heavy weight, no thin strokes. At 640x360, keep letters at least **16 px tall** (about 7.5 cm on a 3 m image), readable from 3 m. Better still: draw text at the full 1920x1080 output on top of the upscaled painting, so it stays crisp while the image stays raw.
- **No detail finer than about 4 px** at 640x360 (about 2 cm on a 3 m image). It will vanish.
- **Safe area:** keep text and faces 5% in from every edge (about 32 px left/right, 18 px top/bottom at 640x360). Edges get cropped, bent, or blurred.
- **Keystone:** line the projector up physically, square to the surface, using lens shift if it has it. Digital keystone resamples the image and softens it further, which hurts text.

**Making one image feel immersive:**

- Darkness is the frame. Nothing else lit, no spill on walls or ceiling, so the image is the only thing in the room.
- Put the speaker **under or behind the image**, centred, so the sound seems to come out of the figure.
- Sub-bass on the floor near the image wall (a corner gives extra bass). The visitor should feel it in the chest when they approach.

## 5. Speaker and audio path

The Pi 5 has no headphone jack. Two routes:

| Route | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| HDMI audio to the projector, then its audio out to the speaker | One cable from the Pi | Many projectors have no audio out, or a weak one; adds lag |
| **USB DAC (sound card) to powered speaker** | Clean, reliable, independent of projector | One more small device |

**Recommendation: USB DAC to a powered speaker.** Keep levels around **75 to 85 dBA at the visitor's position** (check with a free phone sound-meter app), with a limiter in the software so the peaks never jump. This is a music school building: ask about rehearsals and classes nearby, and agree a level and times with whoever manages the floor.

## 6. Pre-showing checklist (Nov 23)

- [ ] Measure the room: wall widths, distances, ceiling height, outlet positions, window sizes.
- [ ] Photograph and mark the fire strobe, pull station and exit; keep all cables and gear clear of them.
- [ ] Get the projector model; look up throw ratio and lumens; fill in the tables above.
- [ ] Micro-HDMI to HDMI cable (the Pi 5 needs **micro**-HDMI). If the projector is more than 3 m from the Pi, use a 5 to 10 m cable or a micro-HDMI adapter plus a long HDMI cable.
- [ ] Official Pi 5 power supply (27 W), power strip, extension cord.
- [ ] USB DAC and the right cable for the speaker (3.5 mm to RCA or XLR).
- [ ] Gaffer tape (black) for every cable on the floor.
- [ ] **Test at 5:00 pm**, the real showing time, with the real lights.
- [ ] Turn the music to performance level and listen for projector fan noise in quiet passages. If audible, move the projector further away or try eco mode.
- [ ] Check text legibility from 3 m and from the doorway.
- [ ] Check where the visitor's shadow lands.

## 7. Questions for you

1. Which projector? Model, ANSI lumens, throw ratio, lens shift, audio out?
2. Which speaker? Powered or passive, inputs, does it have a sub?
3. Can the windows be covered (curtains, film), and who approves it?
4. Can the pendants be switched off or gelled?
5. What time of day are the showings, and what hours is the piece on display Dec 9 to 13? Daytime hours change the whole brightness plan.
6. Which walls are free besides the taupe one, and can a freestanding flat be brought in?
7. Do you want the visitor's shadow in the image, or not?
